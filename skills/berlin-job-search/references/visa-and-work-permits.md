# Visa and work permits

> **Not legal advice.** Residence and employment rules depend on nationality, permit wording, qualifications, occupation, salary, and the responsible authority. Confirm your case with the Ausländerbehörde or German mission and current official sources before accepting work or travelling.

## 2026 salary and route snapshot

Amounts below are gross annual salary unless marked otherwise. They are 2026 values; they can change each year. Salaries are not the only qualification for a permit.

| Route / rule | 2026 requirement snapshot |
|---|---|
| EU Blue Card, regular threshold | €50,700. |
| Blue Card, shortage occupation or recent graduate | €45,934.20; applies to shortage occupations and graduates who completed their degree within the last three years. BA approval is required in the lower-threshold category. |
| First skilled-worker title for applicants aged 45+ | €55,770, or evidence of adequate pension provision. |
| §6 BeschV IT specialist without formal qualification | At least two years of relevant experience in the last five years and a qualified IT job; salary threshold €45,630 unless the employer is bound by a collective agreement and pays under its applicable terms. BA approval applies. |
| Chancenkarte living funds | €1,091 net per month in 2026; proof may be a blocked account, declaration of commitment, or qualifying income. |

Sources: [BA/ZAV Blue Card (2026)](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/vor-ort/zav/working-and-living-in-germany/iss-en/issue-03-2026/blue-card), [Make it in Germany: Skilled Immigration Act](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/skilled-immigration-act/print), [BA/ZAV skilled workers](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/vor-ort/zav/working-and-living-in-germany/iss-en/issue-05-2026/alm-specialists), [§6 BeschV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/beschv_2013/__6.html), [German mission IT specialist information](https://rabat.diplo.de/ma-de/service/2704652-2704652), and [Chancenkarte job-search route](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card/job-search).

The §6 salary value is 45% of the annual pension-insurance assessment ceiling; the statutory exception for employers bound by a collective agreement is in §6(1) BeschV. For actual applications, check the relevant mission and BA/ZAV page rather than a search snippet.

## Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card)

The Opportunity Card permits eligible non-EU/EFTA nationals to search for work for up to one year. The route for recognized skilled workers requires no points. The points route requires six points and a qualification meeting the route's rules, plus at least A1 German or B2 English. Holders may work part-time up to 20 hours a week and undertake trial employment for up to two weeks per employer. The standard financial proof is €1,091 net per month in 2026. See [Make it in Germany: job search](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card/job-search) and [questions and answers](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card/questions-answers).

From 1 October 2026 some missions, including the German mission in the UK, require Opportunity Card applications to be submitted through the [Consular Services Portal](https://digital.diplo.de/chancenkarte). That is mission-specific; check the responsible mission's current instructions before applying. Source: [German mission UK Opportunity Card page](https://uk.diplo.de/uk-en/02/visa/chancenkarte-2659588).

## Other common routes

- **Skilled workers (§18a / §18b AufenthG):** §18a covers skilled workers with vocational training; §18b covers skilled workers with an academic qualification. Both generally need a qualified job offer and the relevant recognized qualification. BA approval may apply depending on case. See [BA/ZAV specialist information](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/vor-ort/zav/working-and-living-in-germany/iss-en/issue-05-2026/alm-specialists) and [Make it in Germany: work visa for qualified professionals](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/work-qualified-professionals).
- **EU free movement:** EU/EEA citizens and Swiss nationals generally do not need a German work visa. Residence registration and other administrative obligations may still apply. Official overview: [Make it in Germany: EU citizens](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/living-permanently/eu-citizens).
- **Qualification recognition:** use [Anerkennung in Deutschland](https://www.anerkennung-in-deutschland.de/html/en/index.php) to find the competent authority; [anabin](https://anabin.kmk.org/) for institution/degree status; and [ZAB Zeugnisbewertung](https://zab.kmk.org/en/statement-comparability) where appropriate. Regulated professions can require a license and language requirements.
- **Students:** third-country students may work 140 full days or 280 half days per year; alternatively, working up to 20 hours per week during the lecture period is possible under the applicable rules. Student-assistant roles may be treated differently. Confirm your residence permit and current official guidance. Source: [Make it in Germany: students and work](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/looking-for-foreign-professionals/entering/admission-labour-market/students).
- **After a German degree:** a third-country graduate can apply for a residence permit of up to 18 months to seek qualified employment; during that search period any type of work is permitted. Apply before the current title expires. Source: [Make it in Germany: prospects after graduation](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/study-vocational-training/studies-in-germany/prospects-after).
- **Blue Card employer change:** notify/consult the Ausländerbehörde before changing jobs. The authority may examine a change during the initial period, and the exact rule depends on when the Blue Card was issued and the current law. **(verify)** See [Make it in Germany: EU Blue Card](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card) and ask the competent authority.
- **Berlin LEA:** Berlin's [Business Immigration Service](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/service/business-immigration-service/) serves employers supporting foreign skilled workers with residence and work matters; appointment access and covered cases can change. See also the LEA's [English contact page](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/en/about-us/contact/artikel.1500715.en.php).
- **§81a fast-track procedure:** an employer can request the accelerated skilled-worker procedure with the worker's authorization and a fee. It coordinates steps with authorities but does not guarantee a visa. See [Make it in Germany: accelerated procedure](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/skilled-immigration-act/accelerated-procedure).

## Questions to settle before a job search

1. Does the current permit authorize the exact work type, weekly hours, and employer? Check the card and Zusatzblatt.
2. Is the role qualified employment, regulated, or subject to a licensing process?
3. Are the degree, institution, and professional qualification recognized for the proposed route?
4. Does the salary threshold apply to guaranteed gross salary, and which allowances count? Verify with the current authority guidance.
5. Does the employer need BA approval or the fast-track procedure?
6. Which German mission or Ausländerbehörde has jurisdiction, and what is its current online process?

Do not send identity documents to an unsolicited recruiter. A job offer is not itself a residence permit.

## Route comparison checklist

Use this as a conversation starter with the authority, not as a self-approval test.

| Candidate situation | Route to ask about | Confirm with an official source |
|---|---|---|
| Recognized academic degree and a qualified offer meeting the current salary rule | EU Blue Card (§18g) | Degree comparability, role/qualification relationship, contract duration, salary components, and whether BA consent applies. | Recognized vocational qualification plus qualified offer | Skilled worker with vocational training (§18a) | Recognition decision, regulated-profession license if relevant, and whether terms need BA approval. [BA/ZAV specialists](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/vor-ort/zav/working-and-living-in-germany/iss-en/issue-05-2026/alm-specialists). |

## Before accepting an offer or travelling

- Read the residence card and every supplementary sheet; do not rely on a recruiter or job ad to interpret work conditions.
- Ask whether the offer's legal employer, work site, start date, hours, and title match the proposed route.
- Separate guaranteed annual gross salary from discretionary bonus or equity when comparing a salary threshold. Ask an authority which components count.
- For a regulated profession, contact the professional recognition authority before committing to an arrival date. A job offer may not replace a required license.
- For a job-search route, confirm the end date and whether the allowed work during the search period is limited; Opportunity Card and post-study permits have different permissions.
- If the permit or application process changes, request written guidance from the competent mission or Ausländerbehörde.

The [Federal Foreign Office](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service) and [Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence) provide current overview pages. Local administrative steps can differ, so check the authority that will decide your application.

## Document-planning checklist

The responsible mission or Ausländerbehörde publishes the definitive list; requirements vary by country and route. Ask whether you need:

- A valid passport and current residence-status documents.
- A signed employment contract or binding offer with salary, hours, job title, worksite, and planned start date.
- Degree, vocational qualification, transcript, and recognition/comparability evidence.
- Certified copies, apostilles, or translations in the form required by the receiving authority.
- A completed employer declaration (**Erklärung zum Beschäftigungsverhältnis**) or other authority-specific form.
- Proof of health insurance and livelihood funds where required.
- Evidence of relevant professional experience for an experienced-worker route.
- Pension-provision evidence where an age-related rule applies.
- A professional license or registration for regulated work.

Do not pay an unverified visa intermediary or send identity scans to a recruiter. Submit documents only through the official mission/authority process and follow its current upload, appointment, and translation instructions.

## Qualification recognition workflow

1. Identify whether the occupation is regulated and which German authority decides recognition.
2. Check the institution and qualification in [anabin](https://anabin.kmk.org/). A database result is not always a formal recognition decision.
3. If needed, request a ZAB Statement of Comparability or use the professional-recognition portal to locate the competent body.
4. For regulated work, ask about license, language, supervised practice, and any adaptation period.
5. Keep the original award, transcript, translations, and authority correspondence together; translate or certify only when the receiving authority requires it.

The recognition portal's [official search](https://www.anerkennung-in-deutschland.de/html/en/index.php) and [ZAB guidance](https://zab.kmk.org/en/statement-comparability) explain different processes. Do not equate a generic academic comparison with a professional license.

## Blue Card and employer-change questions

For a Blue Card discussion, confirm the qualification type, job relevance, contract length, annual gross salary, and whether the role is regulated. Salary thresholds change each year; use the current official threshold for the year the application is decided, not a prior job-ad or forum post.

If changing employer, ask the Ausländerbehörde before resigning or starting the new role:

- Is the new role still eligible for the Blue Card?
- Does the authority need notification, approval, or updated documents during the initial period?
- Does the new salary remain above the current threshold?
- Does the new employer need to provide a revised declaration of employment?
- Are family permits or another residence title affected?

The authority's answer depends on the exact residence title and issue date. **(verify)** employer-change procedure from the current [Blue Card guidance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card).

## Opportunity Card planning

Before using the Chancenkarte, make a budget that covers the full intended stay, health insurance, rent/deposit, travel, and any recognition costs. Confirm:

- Whether the skilled-worker route or points route applies.
- The qualification and language evidence required for that route.
- How the €1,091/month (2026) proof will be documented.
- Which part-time work and trial-employment limits appear in the current permit conditions.
- Whether your responsible mission requires the Consular Services Portal or an appointment.

The official [Opportunity Card questions and answers](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card/questions-answers) and the specific embassy/consulate page take precedence over summaries.
