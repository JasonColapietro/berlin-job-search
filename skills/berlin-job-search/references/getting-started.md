# Getting started: choose the right search path

Answer these before picking boards or drafting applications. You do not need to share private identifiers, documents, or account credentials with an agent.

| Triage question | Why it matters / next step |
|---|---|
| Are you an EU/EEA/Swiss citizen, or a third-country national? | EU/EEA/Swiss citizens generally use free-movement rights; others need a residence title allowing the specific work. See [visa and permits](visa-and-work-permits.md). |
| Are you already in Germany or searching from abroad? | If abroad, decide whether to apply remotely, use a qualifying job-search route, or secure an offer before moving. |
| What is your current residence title and its work authorization? | Read the Zusatzblatt and permit conditions; do not infer work rights from the permit's everyday name. Contact the Ausländerbehörde if unclear. |
| What is your German level (CEFR A1–C2)? What is your English level? | Compare ad requirements with your real level; see [glossary](glossary.md) and [ad analysis](api-sources.md). |
| What field, target roles, and seniority? | Start with a focused role set and two or three title variants. |
| Are you unemployed, in a notice period, or currently employed? | If employment is ending, check the Agentur für Arbeit's registration deadlines immediately. |
| Which cities or regions? Is remote work acceptable? | Pick one primary city, one or two alternatives, and a commuting radius; see [cities](cities-and-regions.md). |
| What is your minimum annual gross salary and acceptable contract type? | Set a realistic range using [salary and compensation](salary-and-compensation.md), not net pay guesses. |

## A simple routing map

- **Moving to Germany / permit unclear:** begin with [visa and work permits](visa-and-work-permits.md); check recognition and official authorities before making travel or employment decisions.
- **Already resident and actively searching:** prepare a German- and/or English-language profile, select the boards in [job sources](job-sources.md), and use the scripts in `scripts/`.
- **German below B2:** prioritize postings whose language is English or explicitly say German is optional; treat inferred language signals as uncertain.
- **Regulated occupation or non-EU qualification:** check recognition with the relevant authority before assuming a qualification is equivalent.
- **Unemployed or job ending soon:** read [unemployment and support](unemployment-and-support.md) and register on time.
- **First German application:** use the CV and letter templates in `assets/`, then read [applications in Germany](applications-germany.md).
- **Offer in hand:** check the terms with [interviews and offers](interviews-and-offers.md) and compare gross compensation, not just monthly net.

## Triage by work authorization

| Situation | Search implication | What to verify first |
|---|---|---|
| EU/EEA/Swiss citizen | Search and interview like a local candidate; the main friction is usually relocation and administration. | Confirm your country-specific free-movement and registration obligations with official sources. [EU citizen guidance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/living-permanently/eu-citizens). |
| Third-country national already in Germany | Search only roles compatible with the permit conditions currently printed on your residence title and Zusatzblatt. | Employer, job type, hours, start date, and any employer-specific restriction. Ask the Ausländerbehörde before changing status or employer. |
| Third-country national abroad with a job offer | A signed offer may support a work-visa or Blue Card application, but does not itself authorize work. | Whether the job qualifies, whether recognition or a license is needed, salary threshold, and responsible German mission. See [official visa types](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types). |
| Third-country national abroad without an offer | Compare a qualifying job-search route with applying remotely from abroad. | Opportunity Card eligibility, funds, language/qualification route, and mission-specific process. [Opportunity Card](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card/job-search). |

Do not put passport, permit, or tax identifiers in a public profile or an unsolicited recruiter message. Share only what the verified employer needs at the appropriate hiring stage.

## Triage by search urgency

| Status | First action |
|---|---|
| Employed, no notice yet | Pick target roles, update a truthful CV and two language profiles, then set a sustainable weekly search cadence. |
| Notice period has started | Confirm the contract's last working day and notice terms; register as arbeitsuchend on time even while employed. |
| Contract ends within three months | Register immediately with the Agentur für Arbeit and ask whether you need an appointment or eServices identity check. |
| Already unemployed | Register as arbeitslos by the first day without work and check benefit eligibility; the earlier job-seeker registration does not replace it. |
| Relocating soon | Separate applications you can do from abroad from tasks that require a German address or residence status; do not invent a German location. |

Official registration details and deadlines are in the BA sections below. Benefit decisions depend on the individual case.

## First week after arriving

This is a planning checklist, not a promise that every service can be completed in seven calendar days.

1. **Find an Anmeldung appointment and register your residence.** Bring the requested identity and housing documents; requirements and appointment availability vary by municipality. The federal [Wohnsitz anmelden service](https://verwaltung.bund.de/leistungsverzeichnis/de/leistung/99115005104000) states that registration is due within two weeks of moving in.
2. **Look for your Steuer-ID.** It is normally issued after registration and sent by post. If it does not arrive, use the [Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) guidance](https://www.bzst.de/EN/Private_individuals/Tax_identification_number/tax_identification_number_node.html).
3. **Check your Sozialversicherungsnummer.** Ask your statutory pension insurer or employer how it will be issued if you have never worked in Germany. See [Deutsche Rentenversicherung: insurance number](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/EN/Versicherung).
4. **Arrange health insurance before employment or study begins.** Compare statutory (gesetzlich) eligibility with private (privat) coverage and ask an insurer or qualified adviser about your circumstances. Federal overview: [Health insurance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/living-in-germany/health-insurance).
5. **Open a bank account if useful for salary, rent, or bills.** Compare account terms, fees, and local requirements; no particular provider is endorsed here.
6. **Keep copies of official notices and deadlines.** Do not email passport, tax, or banking data to an unverified recruiter.

### Practical order of operations

- **First 48 hours:** secure temporary housing, identify the responsible Bürgeramt, and collect the landlord confirmation (**Wohnungsgeberbestätigung**) if required for Anmeldung. Do not pay an unverified landlord or intermediary before confirming the accommodation and contract.
- **First week:** register the address, arrange appropriate health cover, and ask the employer or insurer about the social-insurance number process. A missing number is an administrative issue to resolve, not a reason to send sensitive identity data to a recruiter.
- **After registration:** monitor the postal address used for official letters; request a tax-ID reissue only through the BZSt if the original notice does not arrive.
- **Before payroll starts:** give tax and insurance information directly to the verified employer through its secure HR process, not through a job board message or public profile.

Sources and local processes: [Bundesportal: Wohnsitz anmelden](https://verwaltung.bund.de/leistungsverzeichnis/de/leistung/99115005104000), [BZSt tax ID](https://www.bzst.de/EN/Private_individuals/Tax_identification_number/tax_identification_number_node.html), [Deutsche Rentenversicherung](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/EN/Versicherung), and [health-insurance overview](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/living-in-germany/health-insurance).

## Registering as a job seeker

If you know your employment will end, register as **arbeitsuchend** as soon as possible, no later than three months before the end. If you learn of the end less than three months beforehand, register within three days of learning about it. An **Arbeitsuchendmeldung** is not the same as an **Arbeitslosmeldung**; the latter is due no later than the first day of unemployment. See the [Bundesagentur für Arbeit: register as a job seeker](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/arbeitslosengeld/ihre-schritte-wenn-sie-arbeitslos-werden/wie-sie-sich-arbeitsuchend-melden) and [register as unemployed](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/arbeitslosengeld/ihre-schritte-wenn-sie-arbeitslos-werden/wie-sie-sich-arbeitslos-melden).

If a deadline may already have passed, contact the Agentur für Arbeit now; do not rely on this summary to decide benefit entitlement.

### Search starter checklist

- [ ] Choose one or two target titles and a seniority band; keep adjacent titles in a separate secondary list.
- [ ] Choose the primary city, acceptable radius, and whether a move is realistic.
- [ ] Decide whether remote, hybrid, or on-site work is acceptable; check whether remote means Germany-only.
- [ ] Record permit status and work restrictions privately; do not infer authorization from nationality or an employer's job-ad wording.
- [ ] Choose an annual gross minimum and a negotiable range before discussing salary.
- [ ] Select German, English, or bilingual CV/cover-letter versions that match the actual role.
- [ ] Create a weekly time budget for searching, contacting people, preparing applications, and follow-up.
- [ ] Keep a private tracker and review each new posting before saving or contacting anyone.

For a broad Germany-wide source list, continue to [job sources](job-sources.md). For a Berlin-first search, begin with the [city overview](cities-and-regions.md) and then verify each role on the employer's board.

## A sustainable first 30 days

| Timing | Search task | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–2 | Complete the triage, confirm permit conditions, and choose one primary city plus one backup. | A private profile template and a clear deal-breaker list. |
| Days 3–5 | Prepare one accurate English CV, one German CV if relevant, and a role-specific headline. | Reviewed draft files; no submission yet. |
| Week 2 | Search two or three focused role families and inspect employer careers pages. | A short list of verified openings and a first salary range. |
| Week 3 | Ask for a small number of referrals or recruiter conversations; prepare interview stories. | User-approved, individualized outreach and a story bank. |
| Week 4 | Review response rates and adjust titles, locations, or language filters. | A data-informed search update, not a fabricated CV rewrite. |

Track only the applications and follow-ups you can review. A smaller, current list is more useful than a large export of unverified or already-closed postings.

## If you are moving from abroad

Before booking travel, make a short written plan for:

- **Search route:** apply from abroad, use an eligible job-search residence route, or wait for an offer. Compare routes in [visa and work permits](visa-and-work-permits.md).
- **Qualification:** identify whether your target occupation is regulated and whether recognition is required.
- **Language:** choose roles that match your real German/English levels; do not infer language requirements from the ad's language alone.
- **Finances:** budget for visa fees, insurance, housing deposits, travel, and job-search time without assuming ALG I or Jobcenter benefits.
- **Location:** name a primary city and one or two backups; check that the role's work location and remote policy are compatible with your plans.
- **Documents:** prepare a truthful CV and certificates; keep identity documents separate from routine recruiter outreach.
- **Fallback:** know the contact details for the responsible mission and local authority before a permit or entry deadline.

The [Opportunity Card](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card/job-search) is not a default route for everyone abroad. Confirm eligibility and mission-specific steps before relying on it.

## A weekly search routine

| Day / block | Focus | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Search block | Run a narrow query by role, city, and freshness. | Stop when the source asks for login, CAPTCHA, or an untrusted download. |
| Review block | Read only cards with a plausible title/location fit. | Do not keep a listing whose employer or URL cannot be verified. |
| Employer check | Open the employer's current career page and confirm the role remains live. | Stop if the vacancy is closed or the role identity does not match. |
| Application block | Tailor one CV/letter with accurate evidence. | Ask the user to approve facts and attachments before any send action. |
| Follow-up block | Update the private tracker and review due actions. | Do not send follow-up messages automatically. |

Protect time for language learning and permit or recognition paperwork; the job search is not the only task in a move.
