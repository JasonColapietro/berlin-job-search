# Cities and regions

Industry clusters and language expectations below are general orientation, not guarantees. Confirm each opening's location, German requirement, and on-site rules. Cost-of-living comments are relative; current rent and commute costs vary by district and date.

## Berlin — deepest coverage

Berlin is a strong market for startups, fintech, climate/energy, AI, SaaS, media, and international technology teams; see [Berlin Partner](https://www.berlin-partner.de/en/) for an economic-development overview. Q1 2026 Tech in Berlin tracked 155 software-engineering roles and 39 junior roles across more than 380 startups; senior and management roles were more numerous. [Q1 report](https://www.techinberlin.com/news/berlin-tech-hiring-in-the-first-quarter-of-2026-what-the-numbers-show). On 23 October 2026, Job Drop Berlin reported 2,361 live positions across 401 startups, with 53% marked English-speaking and 17% stating salary (395 roles). [Daily Drop](https://jobdropberlin.com/daily).

English is comparatively common in startup and international-company postings, but customer-facing, public-sector, legal, healthcare, and regulated work often requires German. Employers include Delivery Hero, Zalando, N26, Deutsche Bahn, Charité, BVG, and a large startup/scale-up ecosystem; confirm current openings on their careers pages. Local sources: [BerlinStartupJobs](https://berlinstartupjobs.com/), [Daily Berlin Jobs](https://daily-berlin-jobs.vercel.app/jobs.md) (curated Berlin tech engineering; once-daily update), [Tech in Berlin](https://techinberlin.com/), [Job Drop Berlin](https://jobdropberlin.com/), [pegel.berlin](https://pegel.berlin/), and [Berlin Karriereportal](https://www.berlin.de/karriereportal/). Housing availability and rent can be a major constraint; compare neighborhoods, commute, and current official rent data before relocating.

### Berlin search strategy

- **Startups / scale-ups:** combine an exact title with a sector term (for example, “Data Engineer climate tech Berlin”) and check company career boards directly.
- **Fintech / commerce:** search product, payments, risk, compliance, customer support, and operations separately; the language requirement can differ by function.
- **Climate / energy:** include energy, heat, solar, mobility, battery, and sustainability title variants, then verify technical scope in the job description.
- **AI / research:** distinguish production engineering, applied research, academic research, and policy roles; each has different degree, publication, and German-language expectations.
- **Public and regulated roles:** search the Berlin Karriereportal and the employer's page; read citizenship, license, and language requirements before applying.
- **English-language roles:** confirm whether English is the daily team language or only the posting language; a German customer base can still require German.
- **Senior-heavy market:** check level and ownership requirements instead of assuming every “engineer” role is junior. Use the Q1 report and live role data as snapshots, not forecasts.
- **Housing / commute:** check the exact office location, public-transport route, and required office days; “Berlin” can mean a metro-area site outside the ring.

Keep one Berlin query for the primary role and one for a genuinely adjacent role. Broadening the search across all sectors at once makes it harder to compare fit, language, and compensation.

## Munich

Strong in automotive, mobility, insurance, enterprise software, life sciences, research, and industrial technology. The [City of Munich industry overview](https://www.munich-business.eu/en/business-location/industries.html) covers ICT, mobility, finance, and life sciences; employers include BMW, Siemens, Allianz, Infineon, Munich Re, and TUM-affiliated research. German is common in manufacturing, public-facing, and local-market roles; international engineering teams may work in English. Try [Munich Startup jobs](https://www.munich-startup.de/jobs/), [StepStone](https://www.stepstone.de/), and company career pages. Housing costs are among Germany's highest; check current rents and commuting distance.

## Hamburg

Industries include logistics, aviation, media, maritime, renewable energy, commerce, and consumer brands; see [Hamburg Business](https://hamburg-business.com/en/why-hamburg/top-facts-hamburg) for city-level industry information. Examples include Airbus, Lufthansa Technik, Otto Group, Beiersdorf, and About You. English appears in international and technical teams; German is common in operations and customer roles. Try [Hamburg Startups](https://www.hamburg-startups.net/), [BA Jobsuche](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/jobsuche/), and employer sites. Housing costs can be high near central and waterfront areas.

## Frankfurt / Rhine-Main

Banking, fintech, consulting, data centers, logistics, pharma, and professional services are prominent in the Rhine-Main region. The [City of Frankfurt business overview](https://frankfurt.de/english/international-city/business-in-frankfurt) (HTTP 403 to the automated link check) and [FrankfurtRheinMain industry profile](https://www.find-it-in-frm.de/en/industries-in-frm) summarize the regional cluster. Examples include Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, ECB-related institutions, Lufthansa, and major consulting firms. English is common in international finance; German often matters in client work and administration. Search [StepStone](https://www.stepstone.de/), [Jobware](https://www.jobware.de/), BA Jobsuche, and company boards. The wider region offers commuter alternatives, but travel time and cross-state commutes matter.

## Cologne / Düsseldorf

The region spans media, telecom, insurance, retail, consulting, chemicals, and industrial firms; see the [Cologne/Bonn economic-region overview](https://www.region-koeln-bonn.de/aufgaben/innovation-wirtschaft/wirtschaftsstandort) and the [City of Düsseldorf business overview](https://www.duesseldorf.de/wirtschaft-und-standort). Examples include RTL, REWE, Deutsche Telekom, Henkel, and major public and research employers. German is typical for local customer, media, and public-service roles; global teams may use English. Try [Netlight](https://jobs.lever.co/netlight), local BA/StepStone searches, and employer pages. Düsseldorf and central Cologne housing can be expensive; nearby towns may widen options.

## Stuttgart

Automotive, engineering, industrial automation, software, and advanced manufacturing define the region; the [Stuttgart Region economic overview](https://www.region-stuttgart.de/en/economy/) describes its automotive and engineering clusters. Examples include Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, Bosch, and regional suppliers. German is frequently useful in factory, supplier, and stakeholder-facing roles; global engineering teams may use English. Search [Jobware](https://www.jobware.de/), [INTERAMT](https://www.interamt.de/), and employer sites. Housing and commutes vary widely across the metro area.

## Dresden / Leipzig

Dresden has semiconductor, photonics, research, and manufacturing roles; the [City of Dresden location profile](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/wirtschaft/2024-09-11_Standortbroschuere_DD_2024_en.pdf) describes the technology and research base. Leipzig is strong in logistics, automotive, media, energy, and services; see the [City of Leipzig economic profile](https://english.leipzig.de/science-and-economy/economic-profile-and-clusters). Examples include GlobalFoundries, Infineon, DHL, BMW, and local research institutions. German expectations vary; check customer and production requirements carefully. Use BA Jobsuche, [academics.de](https://www.academics.de/), university boards, and company careers pages. Costs are often lower than Munich or Frankfurt, but availability and salary mix differ.

## Karlsruhe / Darmstadt

Karlsruhe has software, cybersecurity, energy, engineering, and research; the [City of Karlsruhe innovation profile](https://www.karlsruhe.de/wirtschaft-wissenschaft/wirtschaftsstandort/innovationsstandort) describes its research and technology ecosystem. Darmstadt has software, space, chemicals, research, and public-sector employers; see the [City of Darmstadt economic overview](https://www.darmstadt.de/standort/wirtschaft). Examples include SAP, KIT, ESA/ESOC, Merck, and research centers. International research and software roles may be English-friendly; public-facing and administrative roles often need German. Search [Stellenwerk](https://www.stellenwerk.de/), [EURAXESS](https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/jobs), BA Jobsuche, and employer pages. Compare commuter rail and housing by district.

## Role families to compare across cities

| If your target is… | Start with… | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Software / data / AI | Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Cologne, Karlsruhe, Darmstadt | Which team is international, and what German is required for internal documentation or clients? |
| Automotive / embedded / industrial software | Munich, Stuttgart, Dresden/Leipzig, Karlsruhe | Is the role at an OEM, supplier, factory, or engineering consultancy? |
| Finance / risk / compliance | Frankfurt, Berlin, Munich, Cologne | Is the role client-facing, regulated, or tied to German documentation? |
| Logistics / mobility / aviation | Hamburg, Frankfurt, Leipzig, Berlin | Are shift hours, travel, or airport/port site access part of the job? |
| Research / academia / PhD | Berlin, Munich, Dresden, Leipzig, Karlsruhe, Darmstadt | Is funding time-limited, and what language is used in teaching or lab operations? |
| Public service / healthcare | Every region; check local authorities and hospitals | What recognition, license, citizenship, and language conditions apply? |

Treat this as a first-pass search map, not a ranking. A remote role may have country, payroll, or time-zone restrictions; a local employer may hire in English for one team and require German in another.

## Choosing a city

For each target, compare current role volume, German level, salary after housing/commute, partner or family needs, and permit eligibility. Do not choose from a single market snapshot. Use a primary city plus a short radius, then widen only if the role and relocation are viable.

## Comparing living and work arrangements

Do not compare salaries without comparing housing and commute. A higher gross offer may not leave more room after rent, transit, and required office days.

- Search current rental listings in the neighborhoods you would actually consider; do not use a single city-wide average as an offer decision.
- Check the relevant municipality's current Mietspiegel and housing guidance, and confirm whether the posted rent is cold rent (**Kaltmiete**) or warm rent (**Warmmiete**).
- Ask whether the employer pays for a Deutschlandticket/Jobticket, relocation, temporary housing, or travel to an assigned office.
- For shift, factory, or healthcare roles, calculate commute time at the actual start/end hour, not only at midday.
- For remote roles, confirm the allowed country, payroll location, time zone, and required in-person cadence.
- Compare annual gross with realistic monthly net using the salary guide; use a current calculator rather than a remembered tax estimate.

## Choosing a primary and backup city

| Factor | Questions for your shortlist |
|---|---|
| Role volume | Are there current openings for your level, or only adjacent titles? |
| Language | How many current postings explicitly permit English, and what German is needed for customers or authorities? |
| Permit | Does the city have appointment capacity and an employer support route for your residence-title process? Verify with the responsible authority. |
| Housing | Can you afford a realistic location near the office or transit line? Include deposits and temporary accommodation. |
| Network | Do you have professional contacts, alumni, recruiters, or a relevant community in the city? |
| Flexibility | Is the employer open to relocation timing, remote onboarding, or a second office location? |

Prefer a small, reasoned shortlist over applying to every city with a similar job title. The same company may use different salary bands, language expectations, and office policies across its locations.
